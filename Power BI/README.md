### [1] KPI's

##### Total Employee (1470):

The total number of employees in the organization is 1470.

##### Total Attrition (237):
Out of 1470 employees, 237 employees have left the organization, indicating an attrition rate of approximately 16%.

##### Total Male Attrition (150):
Out of the total attrition, 150 employees are male, highlighting that 63% of the attrition comes from male employees.

##### Total Female Attrition (87):
87 female employees have left, contributing to approximately 37% of the total attrition.

### [2] Graphs

##### i. Attrition by Business Travel:

Employees who Travel Rarely account for the highest attrition (156 employees).
    
    102 are male and 54 are female.
    
For employees who Travel Frequently, the attrition count is significantly lower (69 employees).

    39 are male and 30 are female.

For Non-Travel employees, attrition is the least (12 employees).

    9 are male and 3 are female.

Attrition is highest among employees who rarely travel, indicating potential dissatisfaction among this group.

##### ii. Attrition by Marital Status and Gender:

Single employees have the highest attrition:

    73 males and 47 females.

Married employees follow next with a moderate attrition:

    31 females and 24 males.

Divorced employees have the lowest attrition:
    
    9 females only.
    
Single employees are the most likely to leave the organization, possibly indicating issues related to career growth, work-life balance, or job satisfaction.

##### iii. Years at Company by Employee:

The employees with the highest years of service are shown:
    
    EMP1441 has the highest tenure of 40 years.

    Others, as EMP749, EMP1196, and EMP1420, have around 36-37 years at the company.

Long-serving employees might have lower attrition rates; retaining such experienced employees could be crucial for the organization.

##### iv. Average of Working by Gender:

The Average Total Working Years is almost the same for males and females:

    8.26 years for females and 8.23 years for males.

The Average Years in Current Role is also identical:

    4.23 years for both genders.

The average working tenure for males and females does not vary much, suggesting a balanced retention strategy across genders.

The Research and Development department, particularly Laboratory Technicians and Research Scientists, has the highest attrition, indicating potential dissatisfaction or workload concerns.    

##### vi. Attrition by Sales (Job Role):

In the Sales department, attrition is as follows:

    Sales Executives have the highest attrition with 57 employees.
    Sales Representatives have 33 employees leaving.
    Managers have the least attrition at 5 employees.

Sales Executives show the highest attrition, highlighting potential job stress, targets, or other challenges.

##### vii. Attrition by HR (Job Role):

The Human Resources role has an attrition of 12 employees.
Insight: Though the attrition in HR is lower compared to other departments, it still highlights a need to review job satisfaction among HR professionals.

##### viii. Attrition by No. of Companies Worked:

    EMP105, EMP1138, EMP1156, EMP1168, EMP1172, EMP1175.

##### ix. Attrition by Salary Hike (%):

Employees with low salary hikes (24%-25%) have higher attrition:

    Example IDs include EMP243 (25%), EMP1085, EMP224, EMP494, EMP536, EMP599 (all 24%).

Limited salary hikes are contributing to attrition, indicating the need for competitive compensation strategies.

##### x. Attrition by Monthly Income:
